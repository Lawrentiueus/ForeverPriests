Crafting & Gear Optimization

Professions & Weapon Training

The best professions and pairings for Priests in WoW Forever, what each one gives you, campsite buffs, and where to train your weapons.

Professions got a huge upgrade in Forever, with over 600 new recipes. In Classic you could ignore them until max level. In Forever they make leveling noticeably easier, so pick yours early. You get two primary professions, and these are the ones that matter most for Priests.

Best Profession Pairs for Priests

#1 Lawrentiueus's Pick

Enchanting + Tailoring

The classic Priest pairing. Tailoring makes your cloth gear, and Enchanting makes your wands and staves and disenchants everything you craft to skill up. Both feed each other, so you hardly need to buy materials.

  • Best gear while leveling
  • Your own wands from level 1
  • Craft, then disenchant to level both
#2 Runner-Up

Alchemy + Herbalism

Gather your own herbs and brew your own mana potions and elixirs. Leveling Alchemy is usually expensive, and picking your own herbs cuts that cost to almost nothing. It's also a strong gold-maker.

  • Free mana potions and elixirs
  • Herbs sell well while leveling
  • Transmutes for steady gold
#3 For PvP & Raiders

Engineering + Mining

Mining supplies the bars and stone Engineering needs. Pick this for gadgets, goggles and explosives, especially for PvP or raiding. Raiders often swap Mining for Alchemy at max level.

  • Explosives for AoE damage
  • Utility trinkets for PvP
  • Great with Gnome's Engineering racial

The Professions

Enchanting

Crafting · Disenchant your loot, make your own wands and staves

Enchanting turns the green items you find while leveling into dust and essences, which are often worth more than selling the item to a vendor. For Priests it does much more than that, because Enchanters craft wands, staves and off-hands.

  • Your first wand right away: training Enchanting teaches the Novice's Practice Wand, made from vendor materials. The Lesser Magic Wand follows at skill 10 and the Greater Magic Wand at 70.
  • Wands all the way up: Lesser and Greater Mystic Wands (175 and 195), then formula wands such as the Brilliant Wand (275) and Torch of Light (300).
  • Staves and off-hands: Glimmering Staff, Soulstaff, Orb of Mystic Insight and Orb of Souls at 140, then Dreamstaff and Radiant Staff at 220.
  • Weapon oils: Wizard Oils for damage and Mana Oils for regen, from Minor (skill 5) up to Brilliant (300).
  • New material: Mote of Magic. Dust to Motes turns 1 Strange Dust into 3 Motes, which the earliest enchants and the starter wand use.
  • Artisan training: Annora teaches Artisan Enchanting to both factions. She's inside Uldaman and shows up after you clear the nearby scorpions.

Tailoring

Crafting · Make your own cloth gear and bags

Priests wear cloth, and Tailoring makes cloth gear and bags. In Forever it's much stronger than in Classic: Tailors can make full bind-on-pickup leveling sets in versions for each spell school.

  • Gear from level 10: new waist pieces from the trainer at skill 35, made from linen bolts and potions.
  • Sets for your spec: from skill 60 to 125, the Pristine set is the healing version, Shining is Holy and Shadow is Shadow. From 140 to 235, look for Gilded (healing), Radiant (Holy) and Black (Shadow).
  • Filigreed pieces: upgraded versions of some pieces, with patterns that drop from dungeon bosses.
  • Spinning Wheel: the 140+ sets need one to craft.
  • Skyborne patterns: the bind-on-equip Stormsewn set is sold by Vayn Moongaze on Zephras Isle. It's good for alts or the Auction House.
  • Perfect for Enchanting: cloth is cheap, so crafted cloth gear is ideal for disenchanting.

Alchemy

Crafting · Mana potions, elixirs, flasks and transmutes

Alchemists make the potions, elixirs and flasks everyone wants, from leveling to endgame. With over 50 new recipes, many at low levels, it's worth picking up on the way to 60 instead of only at max level.

  • Your own mana potions and elixirs, which matter a lot for a mana-hungry class.
  • Gold from transmutes: long-cooldown crafts like Transmute: Arcanite turn cheap materials into expensive ones. You need a Philosopher's Stone for them.
  • New recipe vendors: many of the new recipes come from Nina Surefire (Azeroth Commerce Authority) and Apothecary Durelle (Durotar Supply and Logistics), bought with Merchant's Favor.
  • Vials: Empty, Leaded and Crystal Vials are sold by general goods vendors. Imbued Vials only come from Alchemy and Herbalism suppliers.

Herbalism

Gathering · Gather the herbs Alchemy runs on

Herbalism lets you gather herbs from nodes around the world. Herbs are always in demand, and in Forever even more professions use them, so they sell well while you level.

  • Easy to start: you can learn Apprentice Herbalism in the starting areas by picking Wild Harvest as an early quest reward.
  • No tools needed: turn on Find Herbs to see nodes on your minimap.
  • Bonus finds: gathering can also give herb seeds and rare materials like Frilled Lichen, Rosecap and Stranglevine.
  • New endgame herbs: Marefoil, Demonsage and Death Lotus.
  • Longer skill-ups: herbs stay worth gathering for skill points a little longer than in Classic.

Engineering

Crafting · Gadgets, goggles and explosives

Engineering builds gadgets, goggles and explosives from metal bars and stone, so it pairs naturally with Mining. For Priests it's mostly a PvP and raiding pick, and the explosives give you the AoE damage the class otherwise lacks.

  • Caster goggles: Spellpower Goggles Xtreme (skill 225, from a drop).
  • Trinkets and gadgets: Minor Recombobulator, Ice Deflector and the new Gnomish Poultryizer (skill 300).
  • Explosives: bombs and dynamite. Raiders like Engineering for these.
  • New in Forever: the Ultralight Goblin Glider, Repair Bots and Reagent Bots.
  • Gnome bonus: Gnome Priests get Engineering Specialization, which makes their gadgets backfire less often.

Mining

Gathering · Ore, bars and stone for Engineering

Mining lets you mine ore nodes and smelt the ore into metal bars. In Forever, Blacksmithing, Engineering, Enchanting and Tailoring recipes all use mining materials, so anything you don't need sells easily on the Auction House.

  • Free to start: it needs no upfront investment, which makes it an easy gold-maker while leveling. You can learn Apprentice Mining in the starting zones by picking Mining for Dummies as an early quest reward.
  • One tool: buy a Mining Pick from any Trade Goods vendor and turn on Find Minerals.
  • Smelting levels you too: smelting ore at a forge gives skill points up to 185. After that, only mining does.
  • New materials: Heavy Thorium Ore, plus new bars (Heavy Thorium, Azerothium and Legionite) and rare finds like Pyrite, Bauxite and Pitchblende.
  • Pairs with: Engineering, which uses your bars and stone. Herbalism also works, so you can gather both kinds of nodes as you quest.

Secondary Professions

Cooking, Fishing and First Aid don't use up your two primary slots, so every Priest can learn all three. Forever made each of them more useful.

Cooking

  • Bonus XP: every cooked food now adds a Well Fed buff that gives 5% more XP from kills.
  • Steady gold: Well Fed only lasts 15 minutes, so food is always in demand on the Auction House.
  • More recipes: lots of new ones, and old recipes were rebalanced to be worth cooking.
  • Camp features: Campfire Kits raise the camp's capacity for a stronger buff, Cookie's Feast hands out Stamina food, and the Iron Oven unlocks advanced recipes.

Fishing

  • Feeds your Cooking: many of the best stat foods are made from fish.
  • Pure profit: it costs almost nothing, so every catch is gold in your pocket.
  • Camp features: Fish Bowl, Fishing Rack and Fishing Hut.

First Aid

  • Healing Potions moved here: in Forever, First Aid crafts Healing Potions, not Alchemy. Handy for the level 10 Tailoring belts, which need potions.
  • Bandages: heal yourself or others without spending mana.
  • New cures: crafted items that remove diseases and bleeds.
  • Camp features: First Aid Kit, Toxin Study and Plague Doctor's Laboratory.
Profession Pairing Tip While leveling, pair a crafting profession with the gathering profession that supplies it, like Herbalism for Alchemy or Mining for Engineering. Once you're level 60 and settled, you can drop the gathering profession for a second crafting one.

Campsites

How camping works in WoW Forever, how to unlock it, and which camp buffs matter most for Priests.

What Is Camping?

Camping is a new system in Forever that sits alongside your professions. Light a campfire anywhere in the world and other players can stop by and add their own camp features. Each one gives a buff, a vendor, or a crafting station. Sit or craft near the fire for a minute and you get the benefits of everything placed there. If you remember the little Cozy Fire Spirit buff from Classic, this is that idea grown into a full system.

Unlocking Camping

  1. 1Around level 5 you'll get the quest The Great Outdoors. Just /sit at the campfire in front of the quest giver.
  2. 2You're sent to the nearest town to learn Cooking. The Simple Wood and Flint and Tinder for your first campfire are sold by the Cooking trainer.
  3. 3Craft your Basic Campfire. It holds 3 features, but you can only place one yourself, so team up with other players for the rest.
  4. 4You also get a 101 quest for each profession you know. Turn it in to that profession's trainer to learn its tier 1 camp feature.

The Rules

  • Each profession has 3 tiers of camp features, and every tier is stronger than the last. A higher tier can be placed over a lower one.
  • Each craft makes 2 features, and placing one uses it up.
  • Features are tradable, but you need the right profession skill to place one. Anyone can get the buff.
  • All camp features share a 1-hour cooldown. Campfires themselves have a 5-minute cooldown.
  • Sit or craft near the fire for 1 minute to pick up the camp's buffs.

Priest Overview

What each Priest spec does in World of Warcraft Forever, and everything that changed from Classic.

The Priest in WoW Forever

Priests are some of the most flexible casters in WoW Forever. One class covers three jobs: stopping damage before it lands (Discipline), healing it back up afterward (Holy), or wearing enemies down with shadow damage over time (Shadow). Forever keeps the familiar Classic structure, but every tree gets new and reworked talents, and several spells that used to be talents are now part of every Priest's spellbook.

The Priest fantasy has always had two faces: the devoted healer who channels the Light, and the zealot who turns to shadow and the mind. Discipline sits between the two and treats both as tools.

Three Specializations

New for Every Priest

  • Devouring Plague and Fear Ward are no longer tied to your race. Every Priest learns them.
  • Shadow Word: Death joins the Priest toolkit.
  • Divine Spirit moved out of the Discipline tree and is now a regular spell.
  • New priest racials: each race gets two Priest-only racial spells, and Gnomes can now be Priests.

Proactive Healer

Discipline Priest

Discipline is the proactive healer. Instead of waiting for health bars to drop, a Disc Priest shields, buffs and supports the group so the damage never lands in the first place, drawing on both Light and Shadow to do it. This is the battle-tested Priest who makes the hard calls in the middle of a fight.

To a Discipline Priest, Light and Dark aren't opposites to choose between. They're tools, and you need both. Where a Holy Priest's power is faith made real, Disc takes a practical view: stop the damage before it happens with shields and mitigation, rather than mending wounds after the fact.

Forever gives Discipline a much clearer identity than it had in Classic, with a long list of new and reworked talents. The headline additions are Penance, Renewed Hope and Divine Aegis.

Strengths

  • Excellent single-target healing and damage.
  • Shields make allies much harder to kill.
  • Flexible: heals, deals damage, and supports with Power Infusion.

Weaknesses

  • Harder to play well than Holy.
  • No mobility, so positioning matters.
  • Mana management takes practice.
  • Vulnerable to melee attackers dealing physical damage.

What Changed in Forever

Most of Disc's new talents boost healing and damage together, leaning into the balance theme. Instead of raw healing, the changes give Disc more damage, utility and damage prevention.

New Talents (7)

  • Penance: A channeled volley of Holy energy that either damages an enemy or heals an ally.
  • Power in Light: Smite and Penance hit harder on targets burning with Holy Fire.
  • Twin Disciplines: Your instant spells deal and heal more.
  • Holy Precision: Your Holy spells hit more often.
  • Soul Warding: Power Word: Shield costs less mana and comes back sooner.
  • Renewed Hope: Direct heals crit more often on targets with Weakened Soul, and shorten it.
  • Divine Aegis: Critical heals leave an absorb shield on the target.

Reworked (9)

  • Power Infusion: Now requires Penance, and boosts the target's spell damage and healing.
  • Wand Specialization: Down to 2 ranks from 5, but with a much bigger wand damage bonus.
  • Silent Resolve: Lowers Holy spell threat and shortens stuns, fears and silences on you.
  • Mental Agility: Now also makes Smite and Holy Fire cheaper.
  • Mental Strength: Now raises your Intellect.
  • Improved Inner Fire: More Armor from Inner Fire, and more charges.
  • Improved Mana Burn: Mana Burn casts faster.
  • Meditation: Same job, retuned numbers.
  • Power Word: Shield: No longer stops Warriors from gaining Rage when they're hit.

Removed

  • Unbreakable Will
  • Improved Power Word: Fortitude
  • Force of Will
  • Divine Spirit (now a regular spell every Priest learns)
Reactive Healer

Holy Priest

Holy is the classic reactive healer, built around light, divine power and devotion. A Holy Priest is the Light's faithful servant: when an ally gets hurt, they heal, cleanse and protect until that player is back on their feet.

Followers of the Holy path are known for pulling allies back from the brink, often in ways that look like miracles. Compassion is the heart of the spec, and the Light flows through the Priest into whoever needs it. If you've ever played a cleric, this is that fantasy.

Forever strengthens Holy's group healing in particular. Holy Binding heals you for half of every Flash Heal you cast on someone else, and Prayer of Mending heals an ally when they take damage, then jumps to the next party member.

Strengths

  • Strong single-target and group healing.
  • Useful in every kind of content.
  • Easy to pick up.

Weaknesses

  • No mobility, so positioning matters.
  • Mana management takes practice.
  • Vulnerable to melee attackers dealing physical damage.

What Changed in Forever

Holy is still about direct healing, but it gains new ways to heal several people at once, and it rewards you for mixing up your heals instead of spamming one.

New Talents (3)

  • Prayer of Mending: Heals an ally when they take damage or get a direct heal, then jumps to another party member, up to five times.
  • Holy Binding: Your Flash Heals on others also heal you for 50% as much, with no threat.
  • Litany of Light: Gives mana back when you cast a different heal than your last one.

Reworked (8)

  • Twilight Focus: Was Healing Focus. The protection from cast pushback now works on every spell, not only heals.
  • Improved Healing: Now also makes Penance and Prayer of Mending cheaper.
  • Searing Light: More Holy damage, and Holy Fire's burn can make your next Holy Nova free.
  • Spiritual Guidance: Spirit now raises your spell damage as well as your healing.
  • Spirit of Redemption: While in spirit form, every heal is free.
  • Inspiration: Critical direct heals raise the target's Armor.
  • Blessed Recovery: Can now also trigger from one big hit, not only melee or ranged crits.
  • Spiritual Healing: Ranks and healing bonus retuned.

Removed

  • Improved Prayer of Healing
  • Lightwell (as a talent; it's now a trained spell at 40)
Ranged DPS

Shadow Priest

Shadow is the Priest's damage spec, and it has a vampiric streak: the shadow damage you deal heals your allies. Its toolkit is built around damage-over-time spells (DoTs) that wear enemies down.

Discipline and Holy walk with the Light. Shadow Priests have turned away from it, using dark magic to attack their enemies' minds and twist what they see as real.

Shadow gets some of the biggest upgrades in Forever. Devouring Plague is now available to every Priest, Shadow Word: Death joins the toolkit, and Shadowform has been redesigned to cut the mana cost of Shadow spells in half.

Strengths

  • Very little downtime while leveling.
  • Three group buffs plus lots of utility.
  • At 60, Shadowform and Meditation make mana almost a non-issue.

Weaknesses

  • Mana is tight before Shadowform.
  • No true AoE spells.
  • A turret spec: Mind Blast and Mind Flay need you to stand still.

What Changed in Forever

Shadow's changes aim to make it a true damage spec: more damage, much better mana efficiency, and fewer awkward talents.

New Talents (3)

  • Improved Mind Flay: Mind Flay deals more damage and reaches farther, with a weaker slow.
  • Devouring Contagion: Devouring Plague costs less, and spreads to a nearby enemy when its target dies.
  • Early Demise: Shadow Word: Death crits more often on targets at 20% health or lower.

Reworked (9)

  • Shadowform: +10% Shadow damage, Shadow spells cost 50% less mana, +100% Shadow crit damage bonus, and 15% less Physical damage taken. It now blocks healing spells instead of all Holy spells.
  • Shadow Weaving: Now boosts your own Shadow damage instead of the target's damage taken, stacking up to 5 times.
  • Vampiric Embrace: Your Shadow damage heals the party, and enemies with it on can trigger Spirit Tap when they die.
  • Spirit Tap: While active, lets your mana keep regenerating as you cast.
  • Silence: Now also interrupts a spell being cast.
  • Darkness: Raises your Shadow damage.
  • Shadow Reach: Longer range on offensive Shadow spells.
  • Shadow Affinity: Lowers the threat from Shadow spells.
  • Mind Flay: Still a channeled damage-and-slow, now upgradeable with Improved Mind Flay.

Removed

  • Improved Vampiric Embrace

PvE Rotations

Priest Rotations

Healing priorities, damage rotations and key tips for every Priest spec in WoW Forever.

Healer

Discipline Priest Rotation

Your main job is healing, so the healing priority comes first. When nobody needs you, or when you're solo, switch to the damage rotation. Either way, the real skill is making your mana last through long fights.

Healing Priority

  1. 1Power Word: Shield to buy time while a slower heal is casting.
  2. 2Penance for fast healing. Its first tick lands instantly, so it doubles as your emergency button.
  3. 3Lesser Heal for a small, quick top-up.
  4. 4Heal as your main heal when you can afford a few seconds of casting.
  5. 5Renew for steady chip damage, or to cover someone while you reposition.
  6. 6Flash Heal for emergencies only. It's fast but expensive.
  7. 7Prayer of Healing when several party members are badly hurt.
  8. 8Cure Disease and Dispel Magic when a debuff is worth the mana.

Not every debuff needs cleansing. Sometimes letting Renew heal through a damage-over-time effect is cheaper than dispelling it.

Damage Rotation

  1. 1Before combat, make sure Power Word: Fortitude and Inner Fire are up.
  2. 2Right before the pull, shield yourself or your tank with Power Word: Shield.
  3. 3Open with Holy Fire on the farthest enemy, or on your priority target if you took Power in Light.
  4. 4Smite while the enemy closes in.
  5. 5Mind Blast, then Shadow Word: Pain.
  6. 6Penance, if you don't need it for healing.
  7. 7Wand the enemy down.

Finish enemies with your wand instead of Smite or Mind Blast. It costs no mana. Break from the rotation any time you or an ally needs saving.

Cooldowns

  • Power Infusion: boosts an ally's spell damage and healing for its duration. Save it for the moments your group needs the most output.
  • Inner Focus: your next spell costs no mana, and gets +25% crit chance if it can crit. Pair it with an expensive heal.

AoE & Farming

  • Holy Nova is a decent way to hit groups of mobs when farming. It's instant, so nothing can interrupt it.
  • It's expensive, though. Don't rely on it for group healing in dungeons or raids.

Ranged DPS

Shadow Priest Rotation

Shadow's rotation comes down to three things: keep both of your damage-over-time spells (DoTs) up, use your short cooldowns whenever they're ready, and fill every gap with Mind Flay.

Before the Pull

  • Shadowform and Inner Fire on yourself.
  • Power Word: Fortitude and Divine Spirit on the group.
  • Shadow Protection if the fight deals Shadow damage.

In raids, use the group versions instead: Prayer of Fortitude, Prayer of Spirit and Prayer of Shadow Protection.

Single-Target Opener

  1. 1Pre-cast Fade to drop your threat before the fight starts.
  2. 2Pre-cast Mind Blast so it lands as the pull begins.
  3. 3Shadow Word: Pain
  4. 4Devouring Plague
  5. 5Shadow Word: Death
  6. 6Vampiric Embrace
  7. 7Mind Blast
  8. 8Mind Flay

Single-Target Priority

  1. 1Keep Shadow Word: Pain up.
  2. 2Keep Devouring Plague up.
  3. 3Mind Blast whenever it's ready.
  4. 4Shadow Word: Death whenever it's ready.
  5. 5Keep Vampiric Embrace up.
  6. 6Mind Flay as your filler.

After the opener, this is a priority list, not a fixed order. Each time you're free to cast, use the highest spell that's ready.

AoE Opener

  1. 1Pre-cast Fade.
  2. 2Pre-cast Mind Blast on your main target.
  3. 3Shadow Word: Pain on every target you can reach.
  4. 4Devouring Plague on the lowest-health target.
  5. 5Vampiric Embrace on your main target.

AoE Priority

  1. 1Keep Shadow Word: Pain up on every target.
  2. 2Keep Devouring Plague on the lowest-health target.
  3. 3Mind Blast your main target.
  4. 4Shadow Word: Death your main target.
  5. 5Keep Vampiric Embrace on your main target.
  6. 6Mind Flay your main target.

Why the lowest-health target? With Devouring Contagion, Devouring Plague spreads to a nearby enemy when its target dies. If the main target needs to go down fast, put it there instead.

Key Spells

Mind Blast1.5s cast with an 8-second base cooldown (Improved Mind Blast lowers it). Cast it every time it's ready.
Shadow Word: PainYour main DoT. Put it on everything.
Devouring PlagueA stronger, pricier DoT on a 24-second cooldown. One target at a time, unless Devouring Contagion spreads it.
Shadow Word: DeathInstant damage on a 24-second cooldown. If it doesn't kill the target, it costs you 30% of your health.
Mind FlayYour channeled filler. You have to stand still for the whole channel.
ShadowformCheaper Shadow spells and bigger Shadow crits. Starting to cast a heal knocks you out of it.

Mana

  • At level 60, mana mostly stops being a problem. Shadowform halves your spell costs and Meditation keeps half your regen going while you cast, so you regenerate almost as fast as you spend.
  • The exception is AoE pulls where you put Shadow Word: Pain on lots of targets. For those, use a build with Spirit Tap. Vampiric Embrace also triggers Spirit Tap without needing the killing blow.

Threat

  • You rarely pull threat as Shadow. At range, a caster needs 130% of the tank's threat to take aggro.
  • Mind Blast generates extra threat, so it's usually your biggest threat spell.
  • If you do pull, hit Fade and ease off while the tank catches up. A threat meter addon helps.

Always Finish Mind Flay

  • Mind Flay deals its damage in three ticks: at one third, two thirds and the end of the channel.
  • Improved Mind Flay makes each tick hit harder than the last, roughly 100, 120, then 144. Cutting the channel early throws away the biggest tick.

Moving as a Turret

  • Mind Blast and Mind Flay both need you to stand still, so plan your movement around your instant spells.
  • Each instant gives you a 1.5-second global cooldown to move during. Use them in this order: Shadow Word: Pain on new targets, Devouring Plague, Shadow Word: Death, Vampiric Embrace, then refresh an existing Shadow Word: Pain.
  • Haste shortens the global cooldown below 1.5 seconds.

Priest PvP Guide

How to win as a Priest in WoW Forever PvP: specs and roles, talent builds, healing and positioning, dispels, races, gear and macros.

Why Priest in PvP?

Priest has always been one of the strongest and most flexible PvP healers, and Forever makes it better. Penance gives Discipline a fast, safe, efficient heal, Prayer of Mending gives Holy real group healing, and every race now has its own Priest PvP spell. You can heal a flag carrier through a beating, lock down an enemy healer, or play Shadow and pressure from range.

Strengths
  • A huge toolkit: strong casts, strong instants, crowd control, damage and utility.
  • Great solo or in a group, and good positioning can carry a team fight.
  • Power Word: Shield isn't reduced by Mortal Strike effects.
  • Silences, slows and fears on top of deep healing.
Weaknesses
  • Cloth armor, low mobility, and Psychic Scream is your only real escape.
  • Your crowd control has short range.
  • Mana hungry: gear, consumables and know-how all matter.
  • As a healer you get targeted first, and positioning mistakes are punished hard.

Your PvP Toolkit

Healing

  • Penance first, every time it's ready. It's efficient, heals fast, and front-loads its healing, so it still lands most of its value if you get interrupted.
  • Renew and Power Word: Shield early on anyone taking heavy damage, but protect them from dispels first (see Downranking). Shield isn't reduced by Mortal Strike.
  • Flash Heal when someone is low, damage is spiking or casting is risky. It's expensive, but keeping someone alive comes first.
  • Heal and Greater Heal when you're safe and can afford a longer cast. These are your most efficient heals.
  • Against a melee player or anyone with an interrupt, spend your instants before you start casting.

Damage, Control & Utility

  • Help your team burst, don’t tunnel. Keeping your team alive comes first, since trading a teammate for a kill gains nothing.
  • Shadow Word: Pain and Mind Flay for steady pressure. They stop Rogues and Druids restealthing and slow targets so they can't kite your melee.
  • Mind Blast and Shadow Word: Death are your burst when going for a kill.
  • Psychic Scream peels attackers off you, splits a group up, or fears a healer to secure a kill. Jump to stretch its range a little.
  • Dispel Magic often beats raw damage. It removes two magic effects at once.
  • Mana Burn wins long fights against casters and healers, especially with Improved Mana Burn.
  • Mind Control is niche but can force out interrupts or keep a healer busy for a kill.

Positioning

  • Stay at max range: about 30 yards from your damage target and 40 yards from your heal target.
  • Charging in for a Psychic Scream rarely pays off. Avoiding damage and crowd control is usually worth more.
  • Use pillars, walls, trees and hills to break line of sight, gain distance and reset the fight.
  • Many of your best tools have short cooldowns (Shield, Penance, Scream), so stalling a fight lets you use them more often than the enemy.
  • If a fight is going badly, delaying until Scream is ready or until you can get away to drink is a real win condition.

Learning Curve

  • New to PvP Priest? Focus on positioning, keeping your party alive, dispels, and light damage when it's safe.
  • Timing an aggressive Psychic Scream is what separates good Disc Priests from great ones. It's often a bait, but at the right moment it wins the fight outright.
  • Playing passively at range is safer, but too passive and you'll miss kills. That instinct comes with practice.

By Game Mode

Battlegrounds

  • In big fights, stay 30 to 40 yards behind the main fight. Melee have to push through your whole team to reach you.
  • If they keep chasing, back off further and drag them out of their own healers' range.
  • In small skirmishes, everyone is close, so use Psychic Scream more often to make space.
  • Holy Nova (Rank 1 is cheap) reveals stealthers around flags and bases.

World PvP

  • Anything goes, so use everything: terrain, trees, hills, gadgets and consumables.
  • Another enemy can join at any time, so finish fights quickly while you still have mana.
  • Not getting hit at all is better than healing the damage afterward.

Duels

  • There's little room for range or line of sight early on, so play for control.
  • Survive until you can land a full Psychic Scream (not broken by a trinket), then use it to reposition, kite or drink.
  • Disc wins by outlasting: their mana, their health, or their patience.
  • Mana Burn is strong against casters and healers.

Downranking

Dispel Bait

Priests, Shamans and Warlocks can strip your buffs. Put cheap Rank 1 buffs on your target first, so their dispels and purges eat those instead of your real Renew and Shield.

  • Rank 1 Power Word: Fortitude
  • Rank 1 Renew
  • Rank 1 Inner Fire (when no melee is on you)
  • Rank 1 Divine Spirit or Shadow Protection, if you've already cast the higher rank
  • Rank 1 Fade (rarely)

Cheap Utility Ranks

  • Rank 1 Holy Nova: reveal stealthers for a fraction of the mana.
  • Rank 1 Psychic Scream: cheaper when you only need to fear one or two targets.
  • Rank 1 Shadow Word: Pain: breaks Grounding Totem, stops restealths, and fishes for Blackout procs. Blackout procs when Pain is applied, not on its ticks.
  • Rank 1 Mind Flay: a cheap ranged slow.
  • Lower-rank Heal: max-rank Greater Heal usually overheals. Many Priests start at Heal rank 4 and work down to rank 2 as their gear improves.
  • Low ranks get much less benefit from your +healing, so they're not always more efficient. The Spellcoda addon shows each rank's real numbers.

Essential Priest Mechanics

Wands: The Priest Leveling & Mana Economy Engine

Wand stats, quest rewards, and locations reflect currently available launch data and are subject to change as new discoverable content and quests are uncovered.

Why Wands Drive Your Mana Economy

For leveling a Priest, wands are your primary engine for zero-downtime pulling. Passive mana regeneration pauses the instant a mana-spending spell completes and remains locked out for 5 seconds (the 5-Second Rule). Because wand auto-attacks cost 0 mana, weaving your wand at the tail end of combat allows that 5-second lockout to expire mid-fight while you deal continuous ranged damage.

The 5-Second Rule (5SR) Natural mana regen is suppressed while casting and for 5 seconds following any mana expenditure. Wanding lets this clock run down to zero while maintaining damage output.
Zero-Mana Damage Wand auto-attacks act as ranged physical/elemental strikes without incurring GCD or mana costs, keeping your character constantly active while your mana pool refills.

GCD Optimization: The Un-Toggleable Wand Macro

Macro Vault Snippet

The default Shoot icon acts as an auto-attack toggle—spamming it turns wanding on and immediately cancels it. Use this macro to force the game engine to execute wanding on the exact microsecond your Global Cooldown (GCD) expires without accidental cancellations: 

#showtooltip Shoot
/castsequence !Shoot, null
Queue this key ~0.2s before your spell cast or GCD ends for maximum responsiveness.
